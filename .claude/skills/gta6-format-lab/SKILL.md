# GTA 6 Format Lab

## Purpose

Turn a GTA 6 topic into a format that holds attention, then produce it as a short and as a chapter of a long video. Every output must be original: the creator's own script, narration, graphics and analysis, plus generic or original art. Do not use other creators' footage, stripped or restyled videos, or Rockstar assets.

## Hard limits (check before every format)

1. **No third-party footage, restyled or not.** Re-animating, re-narrating or cutting someone else's video keeps their structure and story. A new art style or persona does not change that. Refuse this path and offer the original-script version.
2. **No Rockstar assets or likenesses.** No trailer or screenshot cut-ins, no music from the game, no lookalikes of named characters. Use generic silhouettes or original art.
3. **Label synthetic media.** Any AI-generated voice, presenter or image is disclosed on screen.
4. **Cite numbers.** Every figure on screen has a source line. Estimates are labelled as estimates.
5. **No leaks or spoilers presented as fact.** Rising search terms that depend on leaks or gameplay findings are not topics to build on until an official source confirms them.
6. **Each Short stands alone.** A viewer who has never seen the channel must understand it.

## Workflow

1. **Pick the topic from demand, not hype.** Check trend data (vidIQ keyword research in `rising` mode, trending Shorts, outliers). Record the date and the snapshot in the output, because GTA 6 search data moves quickly.
2. **Verify the claim.** Find an official or primary source. If none exists, frame the video as a question or as your own analysis, and say so.
3. **Choose a format** from the catalogue below. Match format to topic: numbers suit Receipts, dates suit timelines, systems suit Blueprint Teardown.
4. **Write the hook.** The first line must state the answer, the stake, or an open question. Test it against "would a viewer who doesn't play GTA stop scrolling?"
5. **Storyboard four beats** with a timestamp, a visual, and one line of on-screen text per beat. Put the retention device (a reveal, a countdown, a poll, an open loop) in beat 3 or 4.
6. **Build the production plan** using the stack below.
7. **Chain it.** Decide which Shorts are chapters of one long video, and make each Short end with a question the next chapter answers.
8. **Output** a format card: format name, hook, storyboard, retention device, production stack, risk tag (Original / Check first / Avoid), and what to measure.

## Format catalogue

| # | Format | Best for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receipts: kinetic numbers and bar charts with cited sources | Cost, budget, scale comparisons | Original |
| 2 | Question-first explainer with a drawn timeline (e.g. "Is it coming to PC?") | Search-driven questions | Original |
| 3 | Case-file collage with redactions revealed one at a time | Speculation, lore, theories (labelled as speculation) | Original |
| 4 | Trading-card versus duel with a guess-first reveal | Comparisons (GTA 5 vs 6, map vs map) | Original, no Rockstar art |
| 5 | Bulletin cut: lower-thirds and ticker on a fictional channel | Breaking-news framing of a verified update | Check first (disclose voice; no real news brand) |
| 6 | Blueprint teardown of a system drawn from public information | How the economy, wanted system, or map works | Original |
| 7 | Prediction ladder, lowest rung first, graded in a follow-up | Predictions and accountability | Original |
| 8 | Pixel route and timer on an original map | Challenges, map knowledge | Original (no copy of the real map) |
| 9 | Lore dossier as ID cards, one field per beat | Characters and factions, with original portraits | Check first (likeness rule) |
| 10 | Two-voice desk with original presenters | Debates and hot takes, not reaction to a single creator | Check first (disclose AI voices; script must be original) |
| 11 | Phone feed with scroll-speed change at the key post | Sentiment, community discourse (invented accounts only) | Original |
| 12 | Chapter spine: each Short is a chapter of one long video | Chaining Shorts into long-form | Original (rule, not a visual) |

## Production stack

- **Remotion**: scene timing from a JSON script; data-driven counts and bars; timelines; ticker and lower-thirds; SVG blueprint paths; split screens; scroll-speed effects. Render the long video and each Short from the same composition, with 9:16 and 16:9 outputs.
- **Higgsfield**: paper, texture and pixel tile plates; original character portraits and scene art. Check the output against the likeness rule before use.
- **ElevenLabs**: narration and announcer voices. Disclose synthetic voices.
- **Orshot**: post cards and thumbnails as templates.
- **vidIQ**: keyword research, trending and outlier checks, thumbnail and title scoring, and post-publish analytics.

## Output format

For each requested format, return one card:

```
Format: <name> (<number>)
Risk: Original | Check first | Avoid — <one-line reason>
Topic and date of the demand snapshot: <topic>, <date>
Verified source: <source, or "none — framed as analysis">
Hook (first line): <text>
Storyboard:
  0:00 — <visual> — <on-screen text>
  0:08 — <visual> — <on-screen text>
  0:20 — <visual> — <on-screen text>
  0:35 — <visual> — <on-screen text>
Retention device: <what holds the viewer, and where>
Production: <Remotion composition / Higgsfield plate / ElevenLabs voice, and disclosure>
Chapter link: <the question this Short leaves open, and the long video that answers it>
Measure: average view duration, % past 3 seconds, Shorts-to-long-form views
```

## Testing

- Publish three formats for one week as 30–40s Shorts, then the same three as a long-form chapter spine with fixed title and thumbnail rules.
- Judge by average view duration, retention past 3 seconds, and the rate at which Shorts lead to long-form views. Not raw views.
- Keep the best format and cut the weakest after three weeks.

## Refusals

Refuse and offer the original-script version when asked to:

- restyle, narrate over, or cut another creator's video;
- use Rockstar trailers, screenshots, music or character likenesses;
- present leaks or unverified spoilers as news;
- hide that a voice or presenter is AI-generated.

Say briefly why: the restyle keeps the other creator's work, and the platform and copyright rules apply to the channel as a whole. Then give the original alternative.
