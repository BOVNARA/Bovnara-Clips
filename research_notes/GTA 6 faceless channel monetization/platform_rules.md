# Platform Rules for Monetizing Short-Form Channels Built on Third-Party Gameplay Footage (as of 2026-11-26)

Scope: YouTube (incl. Shorts), Content ID, TikTok Creator Rewards, Meta (Facebook Reels / Instagram). Sources were read on 2026-11-26. Where a page is undated, that is noted. Secondary sources are marked as such and should not be cited as platform policy.

Bottom line: on all three major platforms, the reused-content rule is judged on the channel as a whole and on the creator's own added value. Permission from the footage owner does not cure it on YouTube. Rights-holder claims (Content ID) are a separate track that can block or demonetize a video on their own. TikTok and Meta both reserve their payout programs for original work.

## 1. YouTube reused content and inauthentic content policy

### Takeaway
YouTube's "reused content" rule (in the channel monetization policies) allows reused footage only when viewers can see a meaningful difference from the original, such as commentary, critique, storyline, or substantive editing. Compilations and reposts without that are ineligible. The rule applies to the whole channel, and permission from the original creator does not make reused content eligible. The former "repetitious content" policy was renamed "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025, with no change to the reused content rule.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's channel monetization policy states that reused content is allowed "if viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video." Allowed examples include "Using clips for a critical review," "Reaction videos where you comment on the original video," and "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary." Source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Not allowed examples include "Short videos you compiled from other social media websites," "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative," "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications," "Content uploaded many times by other creators," and "Content that gets views from mostly non-verbal reactions to your videos without added voice commentary." Source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The policy "applies to your channel as a whole. If you have videos that violate our guidelines, or if we cannot clearly tell that you made the content, monetization may be removed from your entire channel." Reviewers check videos, titles, descriptions, and the channel's About section. Source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator. Reused content is separate from YouTube's Copyright enforcement." Source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Dated changes on the policy page: "July 15, 2025: ... We are also renaming this policy from 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content.' ... There is no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos." Source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The "inauthentic content" section on the same page covers three sub-areas: generic or repetitive content, unsatisfying or off-putting content, and AI personas on sensitive topics. Reused content is a separate sub-section. Secondary source [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected) describes the same three categories as "inauthentic," which matches the primary page.
- Shorts-specific: ineligible Shorts views include "Non-original Shorts, such as unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows, reuploading other creators' content from YouTube or other platform, or compilations with no original content added." Source: [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Shorts-specific: "Shorts over one minute in length that contain claimed content are blocked and not eligible for monetization." Source: [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Shorts revenue requires accepting the Shorts Monetization Module. Shorts views before acceptance do not count. Source: [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Shorts Feed revenue: monetizing creators keep 45% of their allocated Creator Pool revenue. Third-party content is generally not credited against the pool, except music from YouTube's music partners or Dream Track. Source: [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Enforcement: "we may withhold or adjust any of your earnings associated with violations," with payment delays of up to 90 days while third-party rights disputes are resolved. Source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Secondary source [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected) states that YouTube's reviewers look for a "formula," and that reposts with minor edits (cropping, speed changes, captions, music swaps) are not enough. This is advice from a vendor and should not be cited as policy. The primary policy does state that "Songs modified to change the pitch or speed, but are otherwise identical to the original song" are not allowed.

### Inferences
- A gameplay-footage channel that posts clips with a voiceover that adds real commentary, analysis, or a narrative arc is in the "allowed" category. A channel that posts footage with minimal narration, sound effects, or a text overlay is not.
- Because enforcement is channel-level, a handful of clean videos does not protect a channel that also runs templated, unnarrated compilations.
- The "reused" and "inauthentic" tests are separate. A channel can be clean on copyright and still fail reused-content review.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube source defines a numeric threshold for how much commentary or edit is "meaningful." The policy gives examples only.
- The official page does not say how reviewers handle short, heavily edited gameplay clips that are under one minute and not Shorts-monetized. This needs an example-based test.

## 2. YouTube Partner Program eligibility, including Shorts and Creator Rewards

### Takeaway
Current YPP thresholds (no change yet in effect) are 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public long-form watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Official changes announced on 2026-08-10 take effect on 2027-02-01: new applicants will need 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, and Shorts revenue sharing will require 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. YouTube has announced new "incentive" programs for channels below that Shorts threshold, but has not published their terms.

### Cited Findings
- Current YPP eligibility: "1. Get 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months, or 2. Get 1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days." Qualified watch hours from Shorts do not count toward the 4,000 threshold. Source: [YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- Other requirements: follow the channel monetization policies; live in a supported country; no active Community Guidelines strikes; 2-Step Verification; advanced features access; one active AdSense for YouTube account. Source: [YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- Review is "typically in about 1 month," and the channel is reviewed as a whole for policy compliance. Rejected applicants may re-apply after 30 days (or appeal within 21 days). Source: [YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- Inactivity rule: monetization may be turned off for channels that have not uploaded or posted for 6 months or more. Source: [YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- The YPP help page carries a banner: "Starting February 1, 2027, we are introducing updates to the YouTube Partner Program... review and accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027." Source: [YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- Official YouTube announcement dated 2026-08-10: "New creators applying for YPP will need 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days." The update "won't impact creators already in YPP." Source: [YouTube Blog, Aug 10, 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)
- Same announcement: "Beginning February 1, 2027, creators who have 10 million qualified Shorts views over the last 90 days will be eligible for ads and subscription revenue sharing on Shorts. Channels below this threshold remain in YPP and continue earning on long-form content." Source: [YouTube Blog, Aug 10, 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)
- Same announcement: for channels under 10M Shorts views, "these initiatives introduce new ways to earn based on hitting certain milestones, like bonuses for YouTube Shopping, incentives for brand deals, and earnings boosts for starting and growing trends. We'll share more details soon." Source: [YouTube Blog, Aug 10, 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)
- Shorts policy page on the 2027 rule: "Starting February 1, 2027, to be eligible to earn a share of Shorts ad revenue from the Creator Pool and YouTube Premium revenue for each month, you will need to have at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days." Source: [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- From 2027, Shorts ads targeted to 5 or fewer channels will pay direct revenue, with creators keeping 45% of the net. Source: [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Secondary source [MediaPost, Aug 11, 2026](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417147/youtube-doubles-eligibility-requirements-for-creat.html) reports the same new entry thresholds, consistent with the primary blog post.

### Inferences
- For a new faceless gameplay channel, the realistic entry path after Feb 1, 2027 is the Shorts path at 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, or the long-form path at 8,000 qualified watch hours. Shorts-only views from non-original content are excluded from the pool regardless of threshold.
- The Shorts revenue share for a channel below 10M views is currently unpublished. Income for a low-view channel likely depends on the new "incentive" programs, whose terms YouTube has not released.
- The official pages still show the old 1,000-subscriber thresholds. Whether a subscriber minimum survives in the new terms is not stated in the official excerpts read, so a team should confirm it in YouTube Studio's updated terms before planning.

### Gaps
- The specific terms of the new "Creator Rewards" or Shorts bonus incentives (Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives, trend boosts): no thresholds found; YouTube says "more details soon."
- Whether the 1,000-subscriber minimum stays in the 2027 entry criteria: not stated in the sources read.
- The exact pre-2027 Shorts revenue rule was not in the Shorts policy excerpt read (the page documents the 2027 rule).

## 3. Content ID on edited gameplay footage: claim options

### Takeaway
Content ID matches uploads against audio and visual reference files that rights holders supply. A match triggers one of three claim outcomes chosen by the rights holder: block, monetize (ads with some revenue share to the uploader, or none), or track. Outcomes can differ by country. Content ID is available only to approved rights holders with a substantial body of original material. YouTube's official pages do not say how well Content ID handles heavy edits, overlays, or stripped audio. A secondary vendor source says it tolerates speed, pitch, and EQ changes and layered voiceovers.

### Cited Findings
- "Using a database of audio and visual files submitted by copyright owners, Content ID identifies matches of copyright-protected content. When a video is uploaded to YouTube, it's automatically scanned." Source: [How Content ID works](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370?hl=en)
- Outcomes: "Blocks a video from being viewed," "Monetizes the video by running ads against it and sometimes sharing revenue with the uploader," "Tracks the video's viewership statistics." Any outcome "can be geography-specific." Source: [How Content ID works](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370?hl=en)
- Content ID is available to copyright owners who "own exclusive rights to a substantial body of original material that is frequently uploaded to YouTube." Claimants who repeatedly make erroneous claims can lose access. Source: [How Content ID works](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370?hl=en)
- Shorts: "Shorts over one minute in length that contain claimed content are blocked and not eligible for monetization." Source: [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Content ID claims are also a cause for withholding earnings: "Examples of violations where we might need to withhold or adjust your earnings include... Content ID claims." Source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Secondary source [Third Chair, Mar 5, 2026](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/content-id-for-youtube-how-it-works-and-what-it-misses) says matching works on fingerprints that survive "speed changes, pitch shifts, heavy EQ" and "voiceovers and sound effects layered on top," and that Content ID "can miss transformed or short uses." This is vendor commentary, not YouTube documentation.
- Secondary source [Foxi Music, 2026](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/youtube-content-id-for-music-guide-monetization/) confirms the monetize/block/track options and says the system "reads audio fingerprints, not license certificates." Vendor blog; cited only for consistency.

### Inferences
- Content ID claims and the reused-content rule are independent. A video can be claimed (and monetized by the owner) with no strike, and the channel can still fail reused-content review. The claim does not create a strike against the channel (per the Reddit thread, which is user commentary and not cited as policy).
- For a gameplay channel, the most likely rights holder is the game publisher, which would choose the claim action. The sources do not say what that publisher's policy is.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube source describes Content ID's behavior against overlays, cropped or mirrored footage, or stripped audio. These are current blind spots.
- Publisher (Rockstar / Take-Two) Content ID and fan-content policy for gameplay footage: not researched; this is out of the platform-rules scope but is likely decisive for a GTA 6 channel. Needs a separate check.

## 4. TikTok Creator Rewards Program: eligibility and originality

### Takeaway
TikTok's Creator Rewards Program requires 18+, a personal account in an eligible country, 10,000 followers, and 100,000 video views in the last 30 days. Only original videos over one minute earn, and each must reach 1,000 qualified For You views. TikTok's US FAQ says original content must be "filmed, designed, and produced entirely by yourself." Its EEA terms use a looser standard ("produced entirely by the creator and/or adds new ideas to preexisting content"). Re-uploads of third-party videos do not qualify under either standard.

### Cited Findings
- Joining requirements: be in an eligible country; be at least 18 (19 in South Korea); have a Personal Account; have at least 10,000 followers; have at least 100,000 video views in the last 30 days; be in good standing with no fraud or repeated violations. Source: [TikTok Support, Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program)
- Supported countries listed on TikTok's creator academy: United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico, Brazil. Source: [TikTok Creator Academy, Join the Creator Rewards Program](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/creator-rewards-program)
- Video criteria (creator academy): "It must be original, high-quality content over 1 minute long." Source: [TikTok Creator Academy, Join the Creator Rewards Program](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/creator-rewards-program)
- Reward threshold: "It must reach at least 1,000 qualified views" (EEA terms); "a video begins earning after 1,000 qualified For You feed views" (secondary, [Creators Agency, 2026](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-2026)). The TikTok creator academy video page is the primary source; it was not fetched directly.
- Originality, US FAQ: "Post original and high-quality content that is filmed, designed, and produced entirely by yourself." Not original: "Duet or Stitch videos" and "Content that contains lip syncs or copyrighted music that plays for over one minute." Source: [TikTok Support, Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program)
- Originality, EEA terms: a video must be "original content and produced entirely by the creator and/or adds new ideas to preexisting content." Source: [TikTok Creator Rewards Program Terms (EEA)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-eea/en)
- Secondary source [Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-eligibility) reports that the video page excludes "Photo Mode posts" and "sponsored content," and that the account must be public. Secondary, not verified on the primary page.
- The program's "Creator Rewards" takes no account of re-uploads explicitly. Re-uploaded third-party videos fail the "produced entirely by yourself" test in the US FAQ. TikTok's FAQ does not state a separate penalty for re-uploads under this program.

### Inferences
- For a faceless gameplay channel, TikTok's reward path is effectively closed unless the videos are the creator's own recordings with substantive new content (commentary on-screen, original gameplay captured by the creator). The EEA "adds new ideas" wording could allow commentary-driven edits, but the US FAQ wording is stricter. Platform terms differ by region.
- The one-minute rule means short clip channels cannot earn from the program regardless of originality.

### Gaps
- TikTok's standalone "reused content" or "unoriginal content" enforcement policy was not located in this research. The Community Guidelines and Copyright Policy were not checked for re-upload penalties.
- Enforcement for re-uploads in the program (removal from the program versus video-level ineligibility) is not documented in the sources read.
- Dates: the TikTok pages are undated in the fetch.

## 5. Instagram (Reels) and Facebook: bonus and originality rules

### Takeaway
Meta's March 2026 originality rules are published for Facebook. The Instagram bonus page separately requires compliance with Meta's Content Monetization Policies, but the Instagram-specific originality text was not located, so the Facebook rules are the only documented originality standard found. Reels that remix third-party clips count as original only when the creator adds something new on screen. Reacting, stitching, narrating existing footage, or lightly editing someone else's post is "unoriginal" and deprioritized. Accounts that post mostly unoriginal content can be deemed non-recommendable and demonetized. The Instagram Reels bonus is invite-only, limited to US, Japan, and South Korea, and excludes aggregator accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Meta announcement, March 13, 2026: "Reels selectively incorporating third-party content (such as remixes or overlays) are considered original when the focus is an on-screen presence from a creator presenting something genuinely new—like fresh information, analysis, or substantial improvements to a storyline. Simply watching along, reacting with facial expressions, stitching multiple clips together, or narrating what's already on screen—without adding anything meaningful—is considered unoriginal and will likely be deprioritized in Feed and Reels." Source: [Rewarding Original Creators on Facebook, Meta Newsroom, Mar 13, 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)
- Re-uploads and minor edits: "Content that is duplicative or involves minor edits to another creator's post will be considered unoriginal and deprioritized. This includes re-uploading posts the Page or Profile had no role in creating, or making low-value changes... like adding borders, inserting captions, and changing the reel's speed." Source: [Meta Newsroom, Mar 13, 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)
- Monetization consequence: "Creators who are not adding substantial creative value will see their content deprioritized, and they may see their account deemed non-recommendable and demonetized, if they continue to post primarily unoriginal content." Source: [Meta Newsroom, Mar 13, 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)
- Appeals: creators can appeal originality decisions. Source: [Meta Newsroom, Mar 13, 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)
- Instagram bonus (Reels), help center page (undated, © 2026): "Only a select group of creators are invited to Instagram's bonus program at this time... For a limited time, Instagram is testing bonuses." Source: [About Instagram's bonus program rules](https://help.instagram.com/708013994693013)
- Eligibility for the Reels bonus: 18+, located and tax-registered in Japan, South Korea, or the US; professional (business or creator) account; "a minimum of 5 million views within the previous 3 consecutive calendar months"; and "not be an aggregator account." Source: [About Instagram's bonus program rules](https://help.instagram.com/708013994693013)
- Reels bonus payout: "only original content" earns. Branded, boosted, and collaborative content is excluded. Content must follow Content Monetization Policies. Source: [About Instagram's bonus program rules](https://help.instagram.com/708013994693013)
- Photo and carousel bonus minimums are per region (US 5M views per month for 3 consecutive months; UK, Australia, Canada and others 500K; Japan 500K; South Korea 1M). Source: [About Instagram's bonus program rules](https://help.instagram.com/708013994693013)
- Invitations expire if not accepted, and the program "may change while bonus programs are testing." Source: [About Instagram's bonus program rules](https://help.instagram.com/708013994693013)
- Secondary: a Meta Creator Blog post, "Rewarding original creators on Instagram," states that 75% of US recommendations come from original posts. Only the search snippet was read; not fetched. Source: [Instagram Creators Blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram)

### Inferences
- A gameplay channel posting reposted or lightly edited footage has no Reels bonus route. The 5M-views-over-3-months bar is also a separate barrier. Even if invited, aggregator accounts are excluded, and a channel built on others' clips is closer to the aggregator pattern.
- For any Meta monetization path, the commentary bar is the one that matters, and it is applied at the account level.

### Gaps
- Instagram's own original-content policy page (creators.instagram.com) was not fetched in full. The Facebook article is the primary Meta source found.
- No Meta threshold was found for Facebook's in-stream or Reels monetization. Facebook's thresholds were not researched.
- Whether Meta's bonus test (Instagram) will open to non-US creators in 2027 is not stated.
