# Rockstar Games policy on GTA content, gameplay videos, streams and monetization (as of 2026-11-26)

Scope: Rockstar's published policies on fan gameplay footage, streams, monetization, mods, and creator programs, with GTA VI (released 2026-11-19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S per Take-Two's release) as context. Sources were read directly where marked "read"; search excerpts and secondary coverage are labelled. Inferences are marked as such. Several sources could not be opened in full (noted under Gaps).

Key dates: Rockstar's fan footage policy was last updated 2025-01-10 and predates GTA VI. Rockstar's Terms of Service were last updated 2025-02-28. Rockstar's Mod Guidelines are dated 2026-09-10.

## 1. What Rockstar's official fan/creator policy says about monetized gameplay videos

### Takeaway
Rockstar's only published fan footage policy permits non-commercial posting of gameplay footage. It defines "non-commercial" as not making money through the footage, which read literally covers ad-monetized videos. No primary Rockstar source explicitly permits monetized gameplay videos, and the policy reserves the right to take material down at any time.

### Cited Findings
- Rockstar support (read): "Generally, Take-Two Interactive does not object to our fans using materials for non-commercial uses in a manner which does not intentionally spoil the plot for others." — [Rockstar Support: Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material) (Last Updated: January 10, 2025)
- Same page (read): "'Non-commercial' means that you don't make money through the game footage you post or use the material as part of a promotion for a product or service. Take-Two Interactive reserves the right to have its copyrighted material taken down at any time." — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Same page (read): Rockstar says "we're happy for fans to upload footage of their gameplay" and that it shares some fan clips on the Rockstar Newswire and social media. — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Same page (read): the policy "is intended to address occasional, non-commercial in-game content use by individual members of the Rockstar fan community. It does not apply to the exploitation of Rockstar IP for film, TV, or streaming distribution; in music videos; advertisements; or publication in books, magazines, or digital publishing." — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Same page (read): "This is not meant to be an exhaustive statement of Take-Two's policy, and we reserve the right to remove content we find objectionable on a case-by-case basis." — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- A Rockstar support reply quoted on a forum (secondary, undated, appears to be from the earlier GTA V era): "We do not provide specific permission for videos." — [YTTalk forum thread quoting Rockstar support](https://yttalk.com/threads/rockstar-games-take2-youtube-monetization-policy.166137/)
- Secondary summary (bo3.gg, not verified against a Rockstar page): Rockstar's "company-wide" footage policy says "Publication of game footage and stills for any commercial purpose is strictly prohibited. Advertising, sponsored, and/or pay-per-view game footage and stills will be presumed to be in violation of this policy." — [bo3.gg GTA 6 creator rules](https://bo3.gg/games/articles/gta-6-content-creator-rules-spoilers-streaming-and-youtube-monetization). I could not find this wording on the support page above, so treat it as unverified.
- Secondary summary (bykron, not verified): Rockstar's "published footage policy describes a limited non-commercial tolerance, not blanket approval for monetized footage or promotional use." — [bykron.com](https://bykron.com/blogs/learn/can-gta-6-make-you-rich?srsltid=AYCX9vKs5kKymK7mm2N8l3QmMwNblLTuDUEx2Ggp8VpHYfEorWyJEP_h)

### Inferences
- Read literally, a video that earns ad revenue is a video where the creator "makes money through the game footage", which places ad-monetized gameplay outside the stated non-commercial permission. The policy does not explicitly address YouTube ad revenue, so this is an inference from its own definition, and secondary sources (Mubert, bykron, bo3.gg) reach the same reading.
- Monetized GTA content therefore appears to run on Rockstar's tolerance ("does not object") rather than an express license. This is an inference, not a Rockstar statement.
- The carve-out for "streaming distribution" is undefined. Whether a monetized livestream falls under it is not stated, so it is unclear if livestreams are covered by the fan permission at all. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No Rockstar primary text found that explicitly permits or prohibits ad-monetized gameplay videos.
- The bo3.gg "presumed to be in violation" wording could not be located on a Rockstar page.

## 2. Does the policy cover GTA 6 specifically, or only earlier titles?

### Takeaway
The fan footage policy does not name GTA VI or any title. It is written for "Rockstar Games material," and it was last updated on 2025-01-10, before GTA VI's 2026-11-19 release. Secondary coverage says Rockstar has not issued a GTA VI-specific creator policy. The Terms of Service and the 2026 Mod Guidelines are also not title-specific.

### Cited Findings
- The support policy's text is generic ("Rockstar Games material", "the game") and does not mention GTA VI, with Last Updated: January 10, 2025 (read). — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- GTA VI was released 2026-11-19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S and is rated M; the release lists no PC version (read). — [Take-Two press release, "Rockstar Games Announces Grand Theft Auto VI Now Available"](https://www.take2games.com/ir/news/rockstar-games-announces-grand-theft-auto-vi-now-available)
- Secondary (bo3.gg): "Rockstar Games has not released a policy specific to content creators for Grand Theft Auto 6, so for the time being, content creators will have to abide by Rockstar's general video policy, which was published on January 10, 2025." — [bo3.gg](https://bo3.gg/games/articles/gta-6-content-creator-rules-spoilers-streaming-and-youtube-monetization)
- The Mod Guidelines dated 2026-09-10 apply to "Rockstar Games' scripts, code, tools, maps..." across titles and say "Rockstar Games creates unique worlds... which form the official continuity of our games" (read). — [Rockstar Mod Guidelines](https://www.rockstargames.com/community-resources/mod-guidelines)

### Inferences
- The fan footage policy and Terms of Service are most likely the operative rules for GTA VI, since nothing GTA VI-specific was found and both apply to Rockstar games generally. This is an inference.
- The spoiler clause is not time-limited. The policy does not say when its spoiler restrictions expire (bo3.gg, secondary, consistent with the text read). A GTA VI-specific spoiler rule cannot be ruled out, but none was found.

### Gaps

## 3. Rockstar and Take-Two DMCA takedowns and Content ID claims against gameplay channels

### Takeaway
Take-Two reserves the right to take down footage and does issue DMCA notices, including to mod videos and leaked material. The most prominent GTA VI streamer copyright incident (2026-11-22) came from a third-party musician's automated claims, not from Rockstar. I found no published data showing how Rockstar handles monetized versus non-monetized uploads.

### Cited Findings
- The support policy says Take-Two "reserves the right to have its copyrighted material taken down at any time," and lists examples that "would result in a take-down notice" (pre-release footage, spoilers, isolated cinematics, in-game TV/comedy, cheating, unauthorized mods/ports) (read). — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- The ToS DMCA section names Take-Two as the designated agent and lists "copyright@take2games.com" for notices, and covers "Content, UGC, Custom Content" (read). — [Rockstar Legal, Section 8.1](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- 2026-11-22 GTA VI streamer claims: Techdirt, quoting Polygon, reports that streamers "were suddenly hit with a barrage of copyright strikes. They all came from a single source: Colombian reggaeton artist Frank 'El Santo.'" It names theRadBrad (14.1M subscribers) and HollowPoiint (5.2M) as affected, and says the claims "apparently had nothing to do with Rockstar and might have been YouTube's Content ID automatic system." — [Techdirt, 2026-11-25](http://www.techdirt.com/2026/11/25/gta-6-streams-hit-with-takedowns-over-in-game-reggaeton-song/) (read). The Polygon original is [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-streamers-copyright-claim-frank-el-santo/); only its headline and description were retrievable.
- El Santo apologized: "I'm not filing these claims manually, and I don't own any rights to GTA 6," and urged creators to dispute the claims. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/musician-responds-as-countless-youtubers-hit-with-copyright-strikes-on-gta-6-videos-3430646/) (search excerpt). Indian Times says YouTube's automated system matched gameplay against his channel, which hosts official music. — [India Times](https://www.indiatimes.com/trending/who-is-frank-el-santo-colombian-singer-apologises-after-gta-6-livestream-triggers-copyright-claims-against-youtubers/articleshow/136109116.html) (search excerpt)
- 2025 Take-Two takedown: Techdirt reports Take-Two "issued a takedown notice on YouTube videos in which one modder shows off his custom map" for GTA V (search excerpt; full article not retrieved). — [Techdirt, 2025-04-02](https://www.techdirt.com/2025/04/02/take-two-dmcas-video-of-gta5-mod-to-for-gta6-map-content/)
- Jan 2025: a channel sharing a GTA IV mod was taken down by Take-Two (search excerpt). — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/309195/20250124/take-two-takes-down-gta-modders-youtube-channel-creators-push-vice-city-next-gen-edition.htm)
- Leak enforcement: Mashable reports Take-Two asked X Corp to remove "Images and videos extracted from an unreleased copyrighted work GTA VI" (search excerpt). — [Mashable](https://mashable.com/tech/take-two-interactive-gta-6-leakers-subpoena-google-x-corp)
- Adjacent non-video enforcement: Take-Two DMCA notices against 2,900+ forks of a GTA VI PC recompilation project, 2026-11-25 (search excerpt, not verified in full). — [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/gta-vi-pc-recompilation-project-leonida-take-two-dmca-2900-forks-nov-25/)
- Secondary (Dexerto excerpt): Take-Two "can remove content, including cutscenes, in-game TV show clips, and mods, after a release. This action is considered a copyright claim." — [Dexerto (Facebook)](https://www.facebook.com/Dexerto/posts/a-musician-has-apologized-after-countless-youtubers-had-their-gta-6-videos-hit-w/1765995785526060/)

### Inferences
- Rockstar's enforcement against fan videos appears targeted at spoilers, isolated cinematics, leaks and mods. The Techdirt author describes Rockstar as having "not been shy" about DMCAs. Whether Rockstar targets monetized uploads more than others is not documented.
- The El Santo incident shows that third-party Content ID claims can hit GTA VI gameplay regardless of Rockstar's policy. A creator's own monetization status does not appear in these reports.

### Gaps

## 4. Terms on modifying or re-editing footage, music, trademarks, and mods

### Takeaway
The Terms of Service are the strictest source. They limit the Services to personal non-commercial use and forbid commercial exploitation or derivative works without express written terms. They define "Custom Content" to include gameplay clips and livestreams, which Rockstar reserves and may exploit. The 2026 Mod Guidelines bar mod monetization outside Rockstar's official Creator Platform. The support policy says nothing about music, and third-party music is not addressed by any Rockstar source found.

### Cited Findings
- ToS 2.2 (read): a "limited, non-exclusive, ... revocable license to access and use the Services ... for your personal, non-commercial enjoyment." — [Rockstar Legal](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- ToS 2.3 (read): no right to "copy..., modify, create derivative works, commercialize, or otherwise exploit the Services (including the Content) ... unless subject to separate, express written terms provided by Rockstar permitting such conduct." It also prohibits using the Services to "develop, train, enhance, or provide source material for, or promote, any Generative AI Tools." — [Rockstar Legal](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- ToS 5.3 (read): "Custom Content includes, without limitation, all content created using Our Tools including in-game assets, maps, screenshots, videos, recordings of in-game audio, gameplay clips, and livestreams. You may only use Custom Content with the Services and/or only as authorized by us." — [Rockstar Legal, Section 5.3](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- ToS 5.4 (read): "Rockstar reserves all rights to and ownership of all Custom Content," with a freely assigned or irrevocable, sublicensable license to exploit it "for any other commercial and non-commercial purpose related to the Services ... without compensation or notice." — [Rockstar Legal, Section 5.4](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- ToS 6.1(3) (read): "You will use the Services for your own personal, non-commercial use. You will not commercially exploit the Services unless subject to separate, express written terms provided by Rockstar permitting such conduct." — [Rockstar Legal, Section 6.1](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- ToS 5.1 (read): UGC "includes ... photos, images, video, or any other audio or audio-visual works" the user creates, and UGC "specifically excludes Custom Content." — [Rockstar Legal, Section 5.1](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- Mod Guidelines, dated 2026-09-10 (read): "Unless permitted via Rockstar Games' official Creator Platform, all modding must be strictly non-commercial." Monetization is allowed only through storefronts approved by Rockstar and under the "Creator Platform License Agreement," which the page links to fivem.net/terms (not verified further). Other rules: do not expand official storylines; do not modify voiceover performances or use a real person's voice or likeness without written permission; do not use third-party IP "including art, characters, music, photos, games, advertisements, brands or logos, real-world locations, etc."; do not use Rockstar IP or logos to suggest endorsement. — [Rockstar Mod Guidelines](https://www.rockstargames.com/community-resources/mod-guidelines)
- Mod Guidelines (read): "Do not make or share mods that serve as a third-party promotion, product placement, advertisement, or sponsored content." — [Rockstar Mod Guidelines](https://www.rockstargames.com/community-resources/mod-guidelines)
- Support policy (read): bans "Promoting Modifications / Ports," meaning distributing or promoting unauthorized mods or ports that violate Rockstar's rights. — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Secondary (Mubert, not verified against Rockstar): a monetized let's play "sits outside that permission," and Rockstar's policy "can't shield a GTA 6 video from a label's DMCA notice, because it speaks only for Take-Two's material. The songs belong to someone else." — [Mubert](https://mubert.com/blog/gta-6-dmca-radio-streaming-content-id)
- Secondary (YTTalk forum, dated): an in-game music issue was raised and the reply was that Rockstar "don't have control over the music that doesn't belong to them." — [YTTalk](https://yttalk.com/threads/rockstar-games-take2-youtube-monetization-policy.166137/)

### Inferences
- Gameplay footage with in-game radio or licensed songs is probably not covered by Rockstar's fan permission, since Rockstar can only grant rights it holds. This is an inference from the policy's scope, and no Rockstar source addresses music directly.
- It is unclear whether Rockstar's "Our Tools" definition in ToS 5.3 covers ordinary in-game recording or photo tools. If it does, every clip recorded with those tools could be "Custom Content" that Rockstar reserves. The text does not say which tools count, so the reach of 5.3 is uncertain.
- The Mod Guidelines apply to mods, not to gameplay footage. Applying them to video editing or commentary is an inference.
- Trademark use: the Mod Guidelines forbid suggesting endorsement by Rockstar. The support policy does not address trademarks in video titles or thumbnails. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No Rockstar statement on third-party music in gameplay videos or streams.
- No Rockstar statement on re-editing, commentary, or reaction content.
- Whether the built-in recording tools count as "Our Tools" under ToS 5.3 is undefined.

## 6. Official creator program or monetization arrangement

### Takeaway
I found no official Rockstar creator or streamer program or monetization arrangement for GTA VI videos. The only licensed monetization route in Rockstar's own documents is the mod "Creator Platform," which covers mods, not gameplay videos. Several social posts claim streamer rules or a Twitch partnership, but I could not verify them.

### Cited Findings
- Mod Guidelines (read): "Rockstar Games' Creator Platform is the exclusive licensed home for our community to monetize their mods." — [Rockstar Mod Guidelines](https://www.rockstargames.com/community-resources/mod-guidelines)
- ToS 6.1 (read): commercial exploitation requires "separate, express written terms provided by Rockstar." — [Rockstar Legal](https://www.rockstargames.com/legal)
- Support policy (read): for licensing, filmmakers and others "can be submitted to copyright@take2games.com for case-by-case consideration"; the policy "likely does not apply to you" if you are a filmmaker, producer, or represent a production company or advertising agency. — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Take-Two's GTA VI press release lists Twitch among Rockstar's social channels. This shows a presence, not a partnership. — [Take-Two press release](https://www.take2games.com/ir/news/rockstar-games-announces-grand-theft-auto-vi-now-available)
- Unverified (Reddit and Facebook excerpts): "Rockstar makes it clear streamers will be able to stream GTA 6 extended look as it's happening as long as they give some type of commentary" (Reddit title). "Twitch partners with Rockstar Games for GTA 6 launch" (Facebook title). Neither was opened, and neither is a Rockstar primary source. — [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/GTA6unmoderated/comments/1vzhl26/rockstar_makes_it_clear_streamers_will_be_able_to/); [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/groups/842291319566989/posts/2172858336510274/)
- Mubert (secondary): "Rockstar hasn't confirmed a streamer mode that removes licensed songs." — [Mubert](https://mubert.com/blog/gta-6-dmca-radio-streaming-content-id)

### Inferences
- No creator revenue share or sponsorship arrangement is documented. Commercial deals require the written terms that ToS 6.1 describes, and none were found.
- Commentary-led, spoiler-free content is the lowest-risk category according to secondary guidance (bundle.social, bo3.gg). That is advice, not a Rockstar rule.

### Gaps
- Rockstar Newswire, Rockstar Social Club announcements, and Rockstar's GTA VI press or creator pages (rockstargames.com/VI) were not opened.
- The Reddit and Facebook streamer claims could not be verified.
